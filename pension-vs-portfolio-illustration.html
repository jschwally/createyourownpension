When $266,586 Equals $1 Million

By John Schwalenberg | November 2026

It is easy to assume that guaranteed lifetime income takes a large portfolio. Twins Hope and Helen show why it doesn’t. At 55, each had $500,000 saved and the same goal: $40,000 a year in income starting at 65.

Here is what it took each of them to get there.

Helen’s Pension Strategy
Deposit at age 55$266,586
Rollup rate8% a year for 10 years
Income base at age 65$575,539
Payout rate at age 65 (single life)6.95%
Guaranteed income$40,000 a year, for life
Hope’s Portfolio
Portfolio at age 65$1,000,000
Withdrawal rate4% a year
Annual income$40,000 a year

Every withdrawal depends on what the market is doing.

Same income: $40,000 a year
Helen needed $266,586. Hope needed $1,000,000.

How Helen’s Math Works

During the 10 years before income starts, the value used to calculate Helen’s income, her income base, grows at a guaranteed rollup rate. At 65, her payout rate is applied to that income base:

$266,586 × 8% rollup for 10 years = $575,539 income base
$575,539 × 6.95% payout = $40,000 a year, for life

The income base is not cash she can withdraw. It is the number her lifetime income is calculated from. (Accumulation Value vs. Benefit Base)

The Part People Miss

Helen didn’t give up growth to get her guaranteed income. She kept the other $233,414 of her savings invested, and by 65 it had grown to $466,828. Her income was covered, and her portfolio could stay invested for everything else.
