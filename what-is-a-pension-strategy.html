Pension Strategy Mechanics

What a Pension Strategy Actually Is

By John Schwalenberg  |  November 2026

The Pension Strategy is our name for a specific way of using a Fixed Indexed Annuity with an income rider attached. Its job is simple: create a guaranteed monthly paycheck, for the rest of your life, that covers your essential expenses.

The Two Parts

The Fixed Indexed Annuity is the foundation. Your money is never invested in the market, so it is protected from market losses. Instead, when the underlying index, such as the S&P 500, goes up over the year, your account is credited with a portion of those gains. When the index goes down, you are credited nothing for that year, but you do not lose money to the decline. (What Is a Fixed Indexed Annuity?)

The income rider is what turns it into a pension. It guarantees a set amount of income every year for life, no matter how long you live or what the market does. (How an Income Rider Actually Works)

How It Works, Start to Finish

You allocate a portion of your retirement savings, not all of it. Ideally, you then give it time: during a deferral period, typically 5 to 10 years, the value used to calculate your income grows at a guaranteed rollup rate. When you are ready, you turn on income, and the insurance company pays you every month for life. With an income rider, the account remains yours the whole time.

If you need income sooner, that is an option too. Income can start soon after purchase, either from the income rider or from an immediate annuity, which begins paying you right away. It is not ideal: skipping the deferral period means a smaller monthly paycheck from the same amount of money, and an immediate annuity typically gives up the account value in exchange for the income. (Annuitizing vs. an Income Rider)

That is why the strongest Pension Strategy defers when possible, then stays the course, with no withdrawals and no surrender.

Features That Can Make It Work Harder

Income doubler for health care. Some income riders increase your payments, often doubling them, for a limited time, typically 2 to 5 years, if you can no longer perform two of the six activities of daily living or are confined to a care facility. Qualifying conditions and waiting periods vary by contract, and availability varies by state.

A death benefit for your family. Your income payments come out of the annuity’s accumulation value, which is the actual account balance. If you pass away before that value has been used up, whatever remains goes to your beneficiaries, not to the insurance company. And if the accumulation value does reach zero during your lifetime, your income does not stop. That is the guarantee. (What Happens to Your Annuity When You Pass Away)

What You Trade for the Guarantee

An income rider fee. The fee is typically calculated on the benefit base, the value used to figure your income, but it is deducted from the accumulation value. Some contracts calculate it directly on the accumulation value instead. Fee structures vary, so it is important to check the contract terms. (Accumulation Value vs. Benefit Base)

A surrender period, and why free withdrawals matter too. For the first several years, taking out more than the free withdrawal amount triggers a surrender charge. But even a free withdrawal comes at a cost: it will typically reduce the income your Pension Strategy pays you, since the income is calculated from a value that the withdrawal lowers. That is why we avoid both if we can help it.

This is not an irrevocable decision. You can still access your money if you truly need to. But the plan works best when you avoid both free withdrawals and surrendering the policy. When your Pension Strategy is structured properly, using only the portion of savings meant for guaranteed income, you should not need to do either. You make the decision once, for income, and stay the course. (Surrender Charges Explained, Free Withdrawals Explained)

Some market upside. You give up part of what you might earn by being fully invested, in exchange for never losing money to a market downturn.

How This Fits Your Pension Strategy

The goal is not to replace your portfolio. It is to cover the bills with income you cannot outlive, so the rest of your money is free to stay invested and enjoy. Which features make sense depends on your situation, and that is what we work through together on a call.
